# Per-stage design choices — A2 Section 7 (knowledge-base stages)

The eight-facet table extended to **the stages we actually built: Stages 1–4** —
**1** Enhancement (built, deliberately disabled), **2** Layout, **3** OCR,
**4** Index (chunk → embed → store). Stage 0 (ingest/preprocess) is folded into **Data**.

Stages 5–9 (Retrieval, Agent, RL/RLVR, Serving, Eval) are **A3 scope and deliberately
absent**. Their packages exist in `src/doc_agent/` as stubs that raise
`NotImplementedError` on purpose; writing rows for them here would claim work we have
not done.

Structure mirrors `forms/A2_form.docx` Section 7: one row per facet, our choice across
Stages 1–4, then why it fits our corpus, what it costs, and what would change our mind.
Every number is reproducible from `notebooks/kb_demo.ipynb` or the config file it cites.

| Facet | Our choice across the knowledge-base stages | Why — fits our corpus · what it costs · what would change it |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem statement** | Turn a 1082-page scanned 1964 print reference (Abramowitz & Stegun, NBS AMS-55, US-Gov public domain) into a citable vector index over **1040 content pages**, with A&S's own printed formula id (`6.1.8`) as the citation anchor. Stages 1–4 own everything up to a searchable index; answer quality is A3's job. Success metric unchanged from A1: **groundedness/faithfulness ≥ 0.90** (`configs/task.yaml`). A2's own gate is narrower — **≥ 60,000 words from our own OCR**, asserted by `data/validate.py`, never read from the PDF's text layer. | **Fits:** a reference work is queried by named function, so the retrieval unit is a numbered formula block, not a page. Anchoring to printed ids makes a citation checkable by a human holding the book, which is what the "explainable" NFR needs. **Costs:** the word floor must be met by OCR we produce ourselves, so a weak reader fails the assignment outright, not merely the metric — we measured **142,836 words**, 2.4× the floor. **Would change it:** a corpus with no canonical numbering (papers, lecture notes) would force a positional anchor, and groundedness would have to be judged span-wise instead of id-wise. |
| **Data** | 1082 PDF pages rendered at **300 dpi grayscale** by `scripts/get_data.sh` → 1050 printed + 32 front matter. `ingest/loader.py` drops front matter, blank versos and pure plates, leaving **1040 content pages**. `ingest/preprocess.py` (Stage 0) deskews, denoises and CLAHEs into `data/interim/`; grayscale is kept, **no binarisation**. Labels: **181 hand-annotated pages** — 122 train (`data/annot/train/`), 20 val (`data/annot/val/`), **39 held-out TEST** (`grading_kit/labels.jsonl`). Split is **by chapter, not by page**: 20 build / 4 val / 5 test of 29, with three disjointness assertions running at import time in `data/validate.py`. | **Fits:** 300 dpi is the lowest setting at which A&S's subscripts and primes survive; grayscale-not-binarised because thresholding destroys the thin strokes separating `ı` from `1` in this typeface. Chapter-level splitting matters more here than it would elsewhere: notation is *chapter-local* (chapter 9 reuses `J`, `Y`, `K` on nearly every page), so a page-level split would leak the exact notation under test. **Costs:** chapter splitting yields far fewer usable training pages than a page split would, and 181 labelled pages is small for a vision model — which is precisely why Methods needs a two-stage curriculum. **Would change it:** uniform notation throughout would make page-level splitting safe and roughly double the labels; a born-digital PDF would skip Stages 1–3 entirely. |
| **Model** | **Stage 1 Enhancement:** none — `enhance: {enabled: false}`. **Stage 2 Layout:** `microsoft/table-transformer-detection`, `score_thr 0.5`, method `projection-profile+tatr`. **Stage 3 OCR:** `facebook/nougat-base`, **fine-tuned** with LoRA, pinned to revision `abfecedb…` in both `vision/ocr.py` and `configs/train_ocr.yaml`; two adapters under `data/models/ocr_lora/` — `curve_n122` (whole page) and `table_ft` (table crops). **Stage 4:** `BAAI/bge-m3` at dim **1024**, index `faiss:flat` (`IndexFlatIP` = exact cosine). All declared in `configs/config.yaml`, which form Section 4 must match verbatim. | **Fits:** Nougat is pretrained on scientific PDFs and emits LaTeX natively, so formulas survive as formulas instead of collapsing into mangled ASCII — it is also the component we reproduce and adapt (Section 3's "Reproduced = yes"). BGE-M3 handles mixed prose/LaTeX, which is what our chunks actually contain. Flat index because 3,543 vectors sits far below the scale where approximate search earns its keep: exact search costs ~16 MB and removes recall tuning as a variable. **Costs:** pinning the revision means no free upstream improvements; flat search is O(n) and would not survive a 10× corpus. **Would change it:** >100k chunks would force HNSW or IVF-PQ and reintroduce a recall/latency trade-off; a handwritten corpus would need a different foundation than Nougat. |
| **Methods** | **Two-stage LoRA curriculum** (`configs/train_ocr.yaml`, run by `training/train.py`): `r=8, alpha=16, dropout=0.05`, target modules auto-discovered across the Swin encoder and BART decoder by `training/adapt.py`. **Stage A** — 695 NIST formula pairs (`data/annot/nist/pairs.jsonl`), synthetically degraded, `lr 5e-5`, batch 4, 4 epochs, **no val split** (an off-distribution warm-up, deliberately not the model-selection signal). **Stage B** — the 122 real A&S pages, `lr 2e-5`, batch 1, ≤16 epochs, **early-stopped on `val_loss`, patience 3**. Degradation is **calibrated to measured A&S statistics**, not guessed: target brightness 198.0 / contrast-std 32.0 (`configs/degradation.yaml`), verified at 196.3 / 32.8 over a 15-page sample. Inference adds the **region-routing hybrid** — `table_ft` applied only to detected table crops — plus `repetition_penalty=1.1` and a degeneracy detector in `vision/ocr.py`. | **Fits:** 122 labelled pages cannot train a vision-language model alone, so Stage A buys volume from NIST's clean formula pairs — and the calibrated degradation is what makes that transfer, since training on clean renders would teach a distribution the real scan never produces. Early stopping on *real* A&S pages means model selection happens on the target distribution, not the synthetic one. **Costs:** ~4.4 h GPU for a full-book pass; two stages is materially more complex than one fine-tune; and Stage A's epoch count is tuned by no metric at all, because it deliberately has no val split. **Would change it:** a few thousand labelled in-domain pages would make Stage A unnecessary; a corpus of clean modern scans would make the whole degradation pipeline pointless. |
| **Design** | Four frozen `pydantic` contracts in `contracts.py` carry every seam: `list[Page]` → `list[Region]` → `list[Chunk]` → `np.ndarray (n, 1024)`. **Contracts are never extended** — `tests/test_contracts.py` fails the build if a field is added. Domain facts are encoded *inside* existing fields per summary Section 3f: `doc_id` **is** the chapter (`ch06_gamma`), the formula id lives **inside** `Chunk.id` (chapter, page, region and formula joined by pipes), and per-chunk `ocr_confidence` + `bbox` go to a **sidecar**, `data/ocr/meta.jsonl`, not the contract. Stage order is fixed in `pipeline.build_knowledge_base()`; three `hooks.run()` seams sit between stages, all wired from the single manifest in `wiring.py`. | **Fits:** the chunk id alone is a complete citation — chapter, page, region, formula — so a downstream answer cites without a database round-trip, which is exactly what "explainable" requires. Keeping confidence in a sidecar lets A3 consume it without forcing every stage to carry a field it does not use. **Costs:** structure encoded in a string id is less type-safe than a real field and is stringly-typed to parse back; frozen contracts occasionally force a workaround where a clean schema change would be simpler. **Would change it:** if A3's agent needed per-chunk confidence at decision time on every call, the sidecar would become a genuine bottleneck and the contract would have to grow. |
| **Development** | `configs/*.yaml` is the only source of parameters — **code reads no literals**. Config is split by purpose: `config.yaml` (serve-time), and `train_ocr.yaml` / `degradation.yaml` / `nist_extract.yaml` (one-shot training and data-prep). **165 tests** (159 pass, 6 skipped) across 11 files in `tests/`, including a **structure lock** (`test_structure.py`) that fails if a file or public symbol is added or renamed. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs `ruff` → `black --check` → `mypy src` (`disallow_untyped_defs = true`, 73 files) → structure, contract, tool and end-to-end smoke tests on every push. Experiment logging is **Weights & Biases through Lightning's `WandbLogger`** in `training/train.py`, `wandb_mode: offline` by default. | **Fits:** three people work in parallel on one pipeline, so the structure lock and frozen contracts are what stop a merge from silently breaking a teammate's stage. Offline-by-default W&B means a clean clone passes CI with no secrets — which is exactly what a grader will do. **Costs:** the structure lock makes legitimate refactors deliberately painful, since every rename is a two-file change; `disallow_untyped_defs` slows early drafting. Note `mlops/tracking.py` exists but is an **unimplemented A3 stub** — the real logging path is Lightning's, not that module. **Would change it:** a solo project, or a stabilised API, would not justify the lock, and it would be the first thing to go. |
| **Deployment** | Indexing is a **batch, offline job**, not a service: `bash scripts/build_index.sh` (which runs `make ingest index`), or `KAGGLE/step30_full_ocr_reindex/run_step30.py` for the full-book path. OCR needs a **GPU** — measured on a Kaggle T4 at **15.26 s/page, ~4.4 h for 1040 pages**; chunk → embed → store then completes in **~13 min**. Every stage is **resumable by construction**: one `.mmd` written per page immediately, plus `data/index/embed_cache.npz` keyed by chunk, so a killed session resumes instead of restarting. Stage B trains at `batch_size: 1` (whole-page images). Output is `faiss.index` + `chunks.jsonl` + `index_meta.json`, **~16 MB, gitignored and rebuilt**, never shipped. | **Fits:** a reference corpus is static — the book does not change — so paying 4.4 h once and serving a 16 MB index indefinitely is the right shape. Resumability is not a nicety: Kaggle hard-kills sessions at ~9–12 h, and the first full-book run was in fact killed at that ceiling and resumed from checkpoint. **Costs:** any OCR change means re-running hours of GPU; `batch_size: 1` leaves T4 throughput unused, but whole-page 300 dpi images will not fit more. **Would change it:** a corpus updating daily would need incremental indexing behind a service rather than a batch rebuild. |
| **MLOps** | Corpus identity is pinned by a **SHA-256 snapshot** — `data/versioning.py::snapshot()` writes `data/corpus_version.json`, hashing the file manifest (names, sizes, mtimes) rather than ~1 GB of pixels. Model identity is pinned by an **explicit HF revision**, not a floating tag. Dependencies are pinned in `uv.lock` **and** `requirements.lock`, both committed — including `numpy<2.0`, added after a NumPy 2.x/`faiss-cpu` ABI break killed a full-book run at the index step. `data/ocr/` (3.6 MB of text) **is committed** so results reproduce on a clean clone; `data/index/` and `data/pages/` are **gitignored and rebuilt**. Failures are recorded rather than dropped: `data/ocr/failures.json` lists all **53 failed pages** with a machine-readable reason. **What we would monitor:** OCR failure rate per page type, mean `ocr_confidence` drift, chunk-count delta between rebuilds, and index size against `n_chunks`. | **Fits:** three contributors across three machines plus Kaggle means "which corpus produced this number" has to be answerable without re-hashing a gigabyte. Committing OCR text but not the index is the deliberate line: text costs GPU-hours to regenerate, the index costs CPU-minutes, so we version what is expensive to lose. **Costs:** manifest hashing detects a changed file list but **not** silently rewritten pixels at identical size and mtime — a real, accepted blind spot, taken because content-hashing 1 GB per run was too slow. The monitoring column is a **plan, not a running system**: `mlops/monitor.py` and `mlops/registry.py` are unimplemented A3 stubs, and `snapshot()` is available but not yet wired into the pipeline as an automatic step. **Would change it:** a corpus from an untrusted or mutable source would justify full content hashing despite the cost, and would make wiring `snapshot()` into every run mandatory rather than optional. |

## The disabled stage — Stage 1, Enhancement

`configs/config.yaml` locks `enhance: {enabled: false, model: "none", type: "none"}`.
`ingest/enhance.py::run()` checks that flag first and returns pages untouched, so the
generative `Enhancer` class is never constructed and its `NotImplementedError` stubs never
fire.

This is a **deliberate A1 trade-off, not an unfinished stub**. A&S is a clean 1964 print
scan with uniform lighting and no bleed-through, so classical preprocessing (deskew,
denoise, CLAHE) already gets the pixels where they need to be. A generative enhancer's real
value is hallucinating plausible detail into badly degraded scans — and on formula-dense
notation, a model inventing strokes it was not certain about is a correctness risk on
exactly the content we are graded for reading. Enabling it is a one-line config flip if a
future corpus needs it; the stage keeps its place in the fixed pipeline order either way.

## Known limitations, stated honestly

- **53 of 1040 pages (5.1%) failed OCR** and contribute no chunks: 6 to
  repetition-degeneration on dense tables (real content, permanently lost), 16 partial
  cutoffs, 31 near-empty.
- **Table-value retrieval is the weak point.** A `"value of J₀ at x=1.2"` query ranked an
  unrelated chunk first and the relevant formula second; worse, the ground-truth value
  table for that range was itself under-transcribed upstream, so two failures stack.
  Formula and definition queries work well — `"exponential integral Ei(x)"` returns the
  correct page and formula at rank 1, score 0.698.
- **Do not quote the raw "24.6% of words indexed" ratio.** It is distorted in both
  directions at once: our numerator is deflated by LaTeX packing (8.9 chars/word against
  prose's ~5–6) while the archive denominator is inflated by its own OCR splitting single
  decimals into several tokens. **Page coverage — 987/1040 = 94.9% — is the honest
  figure.**
